# Realizing triangulations by equal-weight log-concave maximum likelihood

Let $Q$ be a simplicial convex $(d+1)$-polytope and $F$ one of its facets. For $d\ge1$, the deletion of the relative interior of $F$ from $\partial Q$ is combinatorially isomorphic to the optimal triangulation for some finite set of distinct, equally weighted observations spanning $\mathbb R^d$. For $d\ge2$, every regular triangulation of a full-dimensional convex polytope can, more generally, be realized with its vertex locations fixed. Extra observations are allowed and all triangulation vertices are observations. [Robeva, Sturmfels and Uhler, Problem 5.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01910)

## A convex-order criterion

Write $S=\operatorname{conv}X$, $\nu=|X|^{-1}\sum_{x\in X}\delta_x$, and $\mu=f\,dx$, where $f=e^h$ on $S$, zero outside, and $h$ is finite, continuous and concave on $S$. The relation $\mu\preceq_{\rm cx}\nu$ means $\int\phi\,d\mu\le\int\phi\,d\nu$ for all real-valued convex tests $\phi$ on $S$. Suppose $\mu\preceq_{\rm cx}\nu$ and $\int h\,d\mu=\int h\,d\nu$. A competing upper-semicontinuous log-concave density $g$ positive on $X$ is positive and finite on $S$, so $-\log g$ is a valid test. Convex order gives $\int\log g\,d\nu\le\int\log g\,d\mu$. The inequality $\log t\le t-1$ gives $\int(\log g-h)\,d\mu\le \int_S(g-f)\,dx\le0$. Thus $f$ maximizes likelihood. The strict equality condition for $\log t\le t-1$ gives uniqueness almost everywhere, and the standard upper-semicontinuous log-concave version is then determined.

The following local stability fact will supply convex order. On a nondegenerate simplex $\sigma$ with vertices $v_i$, let densities $\mu_\epsilon$ converge uniformly to a positive continuous density $\mu_0$. Suppose
$$
\nu_\epsilon=\sum_i b_i(\epsilon)\delta_{v_i}+\sum_r a_r\delta_{q_r(\epsilon)}
$$
has the same mass and first moment as $\mu_\epsilon$, with fixed $a_r>0$, each $q_r(\epsilon)\in\sigma$ tending to a vertex, and $b_i(\epsilon)\to b_i^0>0$. Then $\mu_\epsilon\preceq_{\rm cx}\nu_\epsilon$ for small $\epsilon$. For a convex test $\phi$, subtract it from its affine vertex interpolant to get a nonnegative concave $u$ vanishing at the vertices. Group auxiliary atoms by their limiting vertex. In group $i$, let $B_i$ be total mass, $z_i$ its mean location, and $A_i$ its average $u$-value. For an empty group use $z_i=v_i$, $B_i=A_i=0$. Concavity gives $u(z_i)\ge A_i$. On $\operatorname{conv}(z_i)$ with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i'$, $u\ge\sum_i A_i\lambda_i'$. The integral of $\lambda_i'$ against $\mu_\epsilon$ over that simplex tends to the original barycentric integral $B_i+b_i^0$, by the affine change of variables and uniform convergence. This limit exceeds $B_i$. Uniformly over test functions, $\int u\,d\mu_\epsilon\ge\sum_i B_i A_i=\int u\,d\nu_\epsilon$. The affine interpolant integrates equally against the two measures, giving convex order.

## Equalizing the weights for $d\ge2$

For a fixed regular triangulation with vertices $v_i$, let $\phi_i$ be its continuous piecewise-affine nodal basis. For heights $y$ set $h_y=\sum_i y_i\phi_i$ and $Z(y)=\int_S e^{h_y}\,dx$. The heights giving a strict concave fold at each interior facet form a nonempty open set. The coordinates of $\nabla Z$ are $\int_S\phi_i e^{h_y}\,dx$. The Hessian is positive definite because its quadratic form is the positive-density integral of $(\sum_i a_i\phi_i)^2$ for $a\ne 0$. Normalize the starting heights to give $Z=1$. Since $\sum_i\phi_i=1$, the coordinates of $\nabla Z$ sum to $Z$. Perturb the gradient to a nearby rational probability vector $w$ and use the inverse function theorem to obtain strict heights $y^0$ with $\nabla Z(y^0)=w$ and $Z(y^0)=1$. Put $f_0=e^{h_{y^0}}$ and $c_{\sigma i}=\int_\sigma \lambda_{\sigma i}f_0\,dx>0$. Then $\sum_{\sigma\ni i}c_{\sigma i}=w_i$.

Choose a large common denominator $N$, so $k_i=Nw_i$ is integral. Keep one observation at each $v_i$, initially allotting its mass among incident maximal simplices as $\delta_{\sigma i}/N$ with all $\delta_{\sigma i}>0$ and sum 1. In units $1/N$, the residual quantities $Nc_{\sigma i}-\delta_{\sigma i}$ have integral sum $k_i-1$. The adjacency graph of incident maximal simplices across facets containing $v_i$ is connected. Take a small ball about $v_i$ avoiding every simplex that does not contain $v_i$. Its intersection with $\operatorname{int} S$ is convex. A path in general position inside this intersection between points in any two incident simplices crosses only facets, all shared by incident simplices. Root a spanning tree. At a leaf with residual $q+r$, $q$ integral and $0\le r<1$, allocate $q$ distinct observations near $v_i$ in the simplex interior. If $r>0$, allocate one on the shared facet near $v_i$, with fraction $r$ credited to the leaf and $1-r$ to its parent. Subtract the latter from the parent's residual. Taking $N$ large makes each initial residual greater than the degree of its tree vertex, so balances remain nonnegative. The root remainder is integral. Shared facets have positive dimension, permitting all auxiliary points to be distinct as they approach their respective vertices. Together with the vertices there are exactly $N$ observations. Each observation contributes mass $1/N$.

Now write the displaced observations as $X_\epsilon$. Its nodal moments $W_i(\epsilon)=N^{-1}\sum_{x\in X_\epsilon}\phi_i(x)$ tend to $w_i$. Solve $\nabla Z(y(\epsilon))=W(\epsilon)$ near $y^0$. Strict folds persist and the density $f_\epsilon$ integrates to one. Holding auxiliary allocations fixed at $a_{\sigma r}$, redefine the allocation of vertex $v_i$ to $\sigma$ as
$$
b_{\sigma i}(\epsilon)=\int_\sigma\lambda_{\sigma i}f_\epsilon\,dx
  -\sum_r a_{\sigma r}\lambda_{\sigma i}(q_r(\epsilon)).
$$
It tends to $\delta_{\sigma i}/N>0$ and sums to $1/N$ over incident simplices, by the nodal moment equations and agreement of basis functions on shared facets. Mass and first moment now agree separately on every maximal simplex. Local stability gives convex order there, and the inequalities add to convex order on $S$. The log-density is affine on each maximal simplex, so its integrals agree. The criterion establishes optimality, with maximal affine regions exactly the prescribed simplices.

## The one-dimensional case and polytopes

In dimension one any chain with $m\ge3$ vertices can be realized. Start with a normalized piecewise-affine log-density of strictly decreasing slopes, in coordinates with initial location and height zero. The two barycentric integrals for an interval of length $\ell$, left height $y$, and height change $\eta$ are $\ell e^y A(\eta),\ell e^y B(\eta)$, where $A(\eta)=\int_0^1(1-t)e^{\eta t}\,dt$ and $B(\eta)=\int_0^1 t e^{\eta t}\,dt$. The ratio $B/A$ increases continuously from zero to infinity since $B/(A+B)$ has derivative equal to the positive variance of $t$ under the tilted density. Thus small perturbations of the positive pairs are realized by recursively choosing the height change and length, preserving strict slopes. Multiply all barycentric integrals by a large integer and round the two exterior contributions to positive integers and the $2(m-2)$ interior contributions to positive elements of $\mathbb Z+\tfrac12$. Normalize by their integral sum $N$. Leave $1/N$ mass at each exterior endpoint, $1/(2N)$ on each side of each interior endpoint, and put the remaining integer numbers of auxiliary observations in interval interiors near the vertices. The same nodal adjustment and local stability apply.

Finally take $F$ in $t=0$, $Q\subseteq\{t\ge0\}$, $p\in\operatorname{relint}F$, and $s>0$ small so $(p,-s)$ strictly satisfies the other facet inequalities. The projective map $(z,t)\mapsto(p+s(z-p)/(t+s),t/(t+s))$ preserves the face lattice. Each nonbase inequality $a\cdot z+bt\le\gamma$ becomes $a\cdot z'+(\gamma-a\cdot p+bs)t'\le\gamma$, with positive coefficient on $t'$. Projection gives a regular triangulation of $F$ by the upper facets. A polygon minus the interior of one edge gives a chain in dimension one. Applying the corresponding construction proves the assertion.
