# A cap meeting every 6-space has at most 58 points

Let $A$ be a cap in $\mathbb F_2^8\setminus\{0\}$ meeting every 6-dimensional subspace, and write $m=|A|$. For any hyperplane $H$, $A\cap H$ has an odd circuit. In fact the linear equations $\ell(x)=1$ for all $x\in A\cap H$ must be inconsistent, since a solution gives a missed hyperplane of $H$. A minimal odd dependency is a circuit of length at most eight. Lengths one and three are impossible, so the shortest is of length five or seven.

For $d=4,6$, a subset of $\mathbb F_2^d$ whose pairwise differences have weights 2 or $d-1$ has size at most $d+1$. Translate one point to zero. The supports of the weight-two points form a pairwise intersecting family of edges, either a star or contained in a triangle. Each weight-$(d-1)$ point misses one coordinate common to all those edges. This gives the bound, including when there are zero or one edges. In dimension four equality gives a translate of
$C=\{e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,e_1+\cdots+e_4\}$: at zero the pattern consists either of the four weight-three points, or the three edges of a full star and the weight-three point missing its center. The ten distinct sums of two different elements of $C$, together with zero, give $C+C=\mathbb F_2^4\setminus C$. Two distinct translates intersect in zero points for shift in $C$, otherwise two points. Three distinct translates intersect in at most one point, since two fixed points determine at most two possible translates containing both. Any nonzero $v\notin C$ is $c_i+c_j$, and together with $c_i+c_k$ for a third $c_k$ it spans a 2-space avoiding $C$.

If $A\cap H$ has shortest odd circuit of length seven, normalize it as $e_1,\ldots,e_6,\sum e_i$ in its span $W$. On a coset of $W$ inside $H$, weights 1 and 6 of pairwise differences are forbidden by the cap condition. Weights 3 and 4 yield an odd dependency of size at most five by adding three members of the circuit, and are also forbidden. Thus at most seven points lie in each of the two inside cosets. For the two outside cosets, use the Cayley graph on $W$ with these seven circuit elements as differences. Its eigenvalues at characters of weight $w$ are $6-2w+(-1)^w$. There are 22 positive eigenvalues and no zero eigenvalues. A coordinate subspace supported on an independent set is totally isotropic for the adjacency quadratic form, so its projection onto the positive eigenspace is injective. Each outside coset has at most 22 points. Therefore $m\le14+44=58$ here.

If $m\ge59$, every $A\cap H$ contains a 5-circuit. Each of the eight cosets of its span outside $H$ has at most five points by the weight lemma, so $h_H=|A\cap H|\ge m-40$. Any tuple of at most three distinct cap points is linearly independent. Counting these ordered tuples inside hyperplanes gives the first three falling-factorial moments of $h_H$. Expressing them as power sums yields
$$
\sum_H h_H=127m,\qquad \sum_H h_H^2=63m^2+64m,\qquad
\sum_H h_H^3=31m^3+96m^2.
$$
For $m\ge60$ the product $(h_H-m+40)(h_H-m+29)(h_H-m+28)$ is nonnegative for each $H$. Its sum is $-32[15+t(t^2-11t+54)]<0$, where $t=m-60$. If $m=59$, then $h_H\ge19$, and some $h_H=19$, since the sum of $(h_H-20)(h_H-30)(h_H-31)$ is $-1524$.

Fix such an $H$, and use $V=W\oplus\mathbb F_2^3\oplus\mathbb F_2$, where $W$ is the normalized span of a 5-circuit $C$ in $H$. The 40 outside points fill each of the eight $W$-cosets with a translate of $C$, given by a function $f:\mathbb F_2^3\to W$. The inside points over $h\in\mathbb F_2^3$ must have their $W$ coordinate in $I_h=\bigcap_u(C+f(u)+f(u+h))$, because all other points are sums of outside pairs or excluded by the circuit at $h=0$. Thus $\sum_h |I_h|\ge19$.

Suppose a 2-space $K\subset W$ disjoint from $C$ makes $\pi f$ affine with $\pi f(u)=Lu+b$, where $\pi:W\to W/K$ is the quotient. The linear map $(w,u,t)\mapsto\pi w+Lu+tb$ is surjective onto the 2-dimensional space $W/K$. For an outside point $(c+f(u),u,1)$ its value is $\pi c\ne0$. For an inside point $(w,h,0)$, write $w=c+f(u)+f(u+h)$ by the condition $w\in I_h$. Its value is again $\pi c\ne0$, because $\pi f(u)+\pi f(u+h)=Lh$. Its kernel is therefore a 6-space missed by $A$.

The constant-difference directions of $f$ form a subspace $L_f$. Each such $h$ has $|I_h|=5$. The other directions give $|I_h|\le2$ by the two-translate intersection bound. If $L_f=\{0\}$, equality in the total bound forces $|I_h|=2$ for all nonzero $h$. Since three distinct translates intersect in at most one point, each such difference takes exactly two values. Adding an affine function to $f$ translates each $I_h$ and preserves the property that $\pi f$ is affine. Make this change to arrange $f(0)=f(1)=f(2)=f(4)=0$. Write $a,b,c,d$ for the remaining values at $3,5,6,7$. The difference sets in directions $1,2,4$ are $\{0,a,b,c+d\}$, $\{0,a,c,b+d\}$, $\{0,b,c,a+d\}$, each of size two. All nonzero $a,b,c$ are some common $v$ and $d\in\{0,v\}$. When $a,b,c=0$ use $v=d$. Then $|C\cap(C+v)|=2$, so $v\notin C$. In a quotient by a 2-space through $v$ avoiding $C$, the normalized $f$ is identically zero.

If $L_f\ne0$, the constant differences add under composition of translations, giving a linear map on $L_f$. Subtract a linear extension to make $f$ factor through the quotient by $L_f$. If that quotient has dimension at most one the map is affine, and any 2-space avoiding $C$ works. In dimension two, up to affine terms the map is $u_1u_2v$. For $v\notin C$ use the 2-space through $v$, with the affine case covering $v=0$. For $v\in C$, in each direction $h$ outside $L_f$ the differences take two values separated by $v$, since $u_1h_2+u_2h_1$ is a nonzero linear form. The two associated translates of $C$ are disjoint. Thus $I_h=\varnothing$ in those six directions, leaving total ten. All cases contradict the 19 inside points or property $(*)$. This rules out 59 and proves the bound.
